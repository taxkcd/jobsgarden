## The role

Senior Software Engineer role at Wagmo focused on advancing their AI-driven pet insurance claims automation system. The role requires ownership of the claims pipeline, improving cost efficiency, accuracy, and expanding automation capabilities using AI and LLM APIs.

Responsibilities:
- Learn our claims automation pipeline in depth and become a core owner, reviewing and shipping changes on your own within your first few months.
- Lower the cost of every claim we process by routing work to the right models, while keeping accuracy at or above where it is today.
- Restructure the pipeline so each stage can be changed, tested, and rolled out independently, with evaluations gating every change.
- Ship new automation that lets more claims resolve without manual review, working with Product to decide what comes next.
- Use AI to read veterinary records and make better coverage decisions on more types of claims.

Requirements (required):
- You’ve shipped an LLM-powered feature to production that real users or real money depend on. You set up evaluations before rollout, versioned your prompts and models, and planned for rollback.
- You’ve designed or substantially refactored a system the business depends on, such as payments or claims, without breaking it, and left it easier to change than you found it.
- You’ve taken over a complex system someone else built and become the person others trust with it. Your code reviews change designs, not just style.
- You’ve made real tradeoffs between model cost and quality, and you can show the numbers that proved quality held.
- You use coding agents and AI review tools every day, and they’ve changed how fast you ship without lowering the quality of your work.
- Strong Python skills. Experience with Django and TypeScript is a plus.

Requirements (preferred):
- none stated

Team and product context: You’ll join the Pet Parents squad and own the claims automation system, partnering with Product on feature decisions.

Work model, location, authorization: Remote role based in Latin America. Must work at least 6 hours of overlap with US Eastern Time.
